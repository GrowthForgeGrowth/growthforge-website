CRO: Find Revenue Leaks
Before Buying More Traffic.

Your website may already have the traffic you need. The bigger opportunity may be finding where qualified visitors drop out of the journey from search and AI discovery to enquiry, demo, or sale.

By Charlie Chan GrowthForge CRO · SEO · GEO

THE DATA

Your traffic is only as valuable as what happens after the click.

CRO becomes significantly more important when acquisition is already generating qualified visitors. The question is no longer simply how to attract more people, but how effectively existing demand moves toward a commercial action.

THE REVENUE LEAK

More traffic doesn't fix a broken conversion journey.

A company can invest heavily in SEO, GEO, paid acquisition, and content while still losing commercially valuable prospects between discovery and action.

01 Discovery

A qualified prospect finds your company through Google, AI search, paid media, referral, or content.

→
02 Intent

The visitor lands on a page because the product, service, problem, or use case is relevant to them.

→
03 Confidence

The website needs to establish relevance, differentiation, credibility, proof, and commercial value.

→
04 Action

The prospect progresses toward the appropriate commercial action: enquiry, demo, consultation, or purchase.

Revenue leakage can occur anywhere between these stages.

When qualified demand reaches the website but fails to progress, increasing acquisition can simply send more prospects into the same leakage point.

THE MOBILE GAP

Your acquisition may be mobile-first. Your conversion journey may not be.

Unbounce's analysis of more than 41,000 landing pages found that 83% of visits came from mobile devices. Yet desktop visitors converted 8% better than mobile visitors.

That gap matters because mobile is often where the first interaction happens. If a high-intent visitor discovers a company through search, an advertisement, or an AI-generated recommendation and then encounters a weaker mobile experience, the business may be losing commercial opportunities before the prospect ever reaches a sales conversation.
