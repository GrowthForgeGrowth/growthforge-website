SaaS Growth Metrics That Actually Matter: 15 KPIs to Track

SaaS growth metrics measure how a subscription business acquires, activates, retains, and expands customers while growing recurring revenue efficiently. This guide explains the SaaS growth KPIs that matter, how to calculate them, and how to use them to make better growth decisions.

GrowthForge•Published September 20, 2026•Updated September 20, 2026•Growth Strategy

SaaS growth metrics measure how effectively a subscription business acquires customers, creates product value, grows recurring revenue, retains accounts, expands revenue, and recovers its growth investment. The core SaaS growth KPIs include monthly recurring revenue (MRR), annual recurring revenue (ARR), retention, churn, customer acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (LTV), conversion, activation, and expansion revenue.

Used together, these metrics for SaaS growth show more than a top-line revenue number. They reveal whether growth is coming from qualified acquisition, successful onboarding, durable retention, customer expansion, or increasingly efficient go-to-market execution.

What are SaaS growth metrics?

SaaS growth metrics are the measurements used to evaluate the health and repeatability of a software subscription business. They connect the full customer lifecycle: acquisition, conversion, activation, recurring revenue, retention, expansion, and efficiency.

They differ from general SaaS KPIs because they focus on the drivers and quality of growth. A general operating KPI may report activity, such as website sessions, total signups, or support tickets. A growth metric connects activity to customer value, recurring revenue, or the cost required to produce that revenue.

They also differ from finance metrics and product-usage metrics alone. Total revenue and cash balance remain essential, while feature usage can reveal meaningful behavior. SaaS performance metrics become growth metrics when they help explain whether customers reach value, convert, renew, and expand at a sustainable cost.

SaaS revenue metrics: MRR, ARR, growth rate, and expansion

Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) and MRR growth rate

What it measures: Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) is the normalized monthly revenue from active recurring subscriptions. MRR growth rate measures how quickly that recurring revenue changes from one period to the next.

Formula: (Ending MRR − Beginning MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: MRR provides a current view of recurring-revenue scale, while the growth rate shows revenue momentum.

How to interpret it: Review the movement behind the result: new MRR, expansion MRR, contraction MRR, and churned MRR. A rising MRR growth rate is more informative when retention and acquisition efficiency remain visible beside it.

Annual recurring revenue (ARR) and ARR growth

What it measures: Annual recurring revenue (ARR) annualizes recurring subscription revenue. For businesses with monthly subscriptions, it is commonly calculated from current MRR.

Formula: ARR = MRR × 12

ARR growth formula: (Ending ARR − Beginning ARR) ÷ Beginning ARR × 100

Why it matters: ARR is useful for annual planning and communicating recurring-revenue scale.

How to interpret it: Use ARR alongside the monthly MRR bridge, which explains whether growth came from new customers, expansion, contraction, or churn.

Average revenue per account (ARPA) or user (ARPU)

What it measures: Average revenue per account (ARPA), sometimes called average revenue per user (ARPU), measures recurring revenue generated by an average active account or user.

Formula: Recurring revenue during the period ÷ Average active accounts or users during the period

Why it matters: ARPA helps explain whether revenue growth is coming from more customers, higher-value customers, or both.

How to interpret it: Define whether the denominator is accounts, paying customers, or users, then use that definition consistently. Review changes with expansion revenue and churn so price, packaging, and customer mix are not interpreted in isolation.

Expansion revenue

What it measures: Expansion revenue is additional recurring revenue from existing customers through upgrades, more seats, higher usage, add-ons, or cross-sells.

Formula: Expansion MRR = MRR added from existing customers during the period

Why it matters: Expansion revenue is a key input to NRR and shows where customers are finding growing value.

How to interpret it: Analyze expansion by customer segment, plan, product usage pattern, and acquisition channel. Review it alongside customer retention and expansion strategy insights rather than treating upgrades as separate from the customer experience that enabled them.

Retention, churn, GRR, and NRR metrics

Customer churn rate

What it measures: Customer churn measures the share of customers that cancel or fail to renew during a period.

Formula: Customers lost during period ÷ Customers at start of period × 100

Why it matters: Customer churn shows account loss and the pressure the business faces to replace customers before it can grow.

How to interpret it: Customer churn does not show the value of the accounts lost. Compare it with revenue churn, cohort retention, and segment-level behavior because retention patterns can vary materially across customer groups.

Revenue churn rate

What it measures: Revenue churn measures the recurring revenue lost from cancellations and, depending on the definition used, contractions during a period.

Formula: Churned MRR during period ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: Revenue churn reveals the value of revenue lost, which customer-count churn can hide when higher-value accounts leave or downgrade.

How to interpret it: Document whether contraction revenue is included in the calculation. Keep the definition stable when comparing periods, segments, plans, or cohorts.

Gross revenue retention (GRR)

What it measures: Gross revenue retention (GRR) measures how much recurring revenue from the starting customer base remains after churn and contraction, before adding expansion.

Formula: (Beginning MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: GRR isolates baseline retention health because expansion revenue is excluded.

How to interpret it: A change in GRR points teams toward losses from cancellations or downgrades. Review it by cohort and segment to locate where the retention experience is changing.

Net revenue retention (NRR)

What it measures: Net revenue retention (NRR) measures how recurring revenue from the starting customer base changes after churn, contraction, and expansion.

Formula: (Beginning MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR + Expansion MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: NRR indicates whether existing customers collectively generate more or less revenue over time.

How to interpret it: Pair NRR with GRR. GRR identifies underlying losses, while NRR adds the impact of expansion. Build retention actions around cohorts and lifecycle signals to improve customer retention, reduce churn, and understand NRR rather than relying only on an aggregate renewal report.

Logo retention

What it measures: Logo retention is the share of customers from the starting customer base that remain customers at the end of a period.

Formula: Customers retained from starting cohort ÷ Customers at start of period × 100

Why it matters: It provides the retained-customer counterpart to customer churn and keeps account durability visible even when revenue expansion masks logo losses.

How to interpret it: Review logo retention beside GRR and NRR. A customer base can show strong revenue retention while losing a meaningful number of smaller accounts, so both account and revenue views are useful.

SaaS customer acquisition and conversion metrics

Conversion rate

What it measures: Conversion rate measures the percentage of people or accounts that complete a defined next step, such as visitor-to-lead, lead-to-demo, trial-to-paid, or opportunity-to-customer.
