SaaS Growth Metrics That Actually Matter: Formulas, KPIs & Examples

SaaS growth metrics measure recurring-revenue growth, customer retention, acquisition efficiency, and expansion. This practical guide explains the SaaS KPIs that matter, their formulas, how to interpret them together, and which metrics to prioritize at each stage of growth.

GrowthForge•Published September 20, 2026•Updated September 20, 2026•Growth Strategy

SaaS growth metrics are the measures used to assess recurring-revenue growth, customer retention, acquisition efficiency, and account expansion. The core categories are SaaS revenue metrics such as MRR and ARR, SaaS retention metrics such as churn and NRR, efficiency metrics such as CAC and payback, and product metrics such as activation and conversion.

Used together, these metrics to measure SaaS growth show more than a top-line revenue number. They reveal whether growth is coming from qualified acquisition, successful onboarding, durable retention, customer expansion, or increasingly efficient go-to-market execution.

What are SaaS growth metrics?

SaaS growth metrics are the measurements used to evaluate the health and repeatability of a software subscription business. They connect the customer lifecycle from acquisition, conversion, and activation to recurring revenue, retention, expansion, and efficiency.

They differ from general SaaS KPIs because they focus on the drivers and quality of growth. A general operating KPI may report activity, such as website sessions, total signups, or support tickets. A growth metric connects activity to customer value, recurring revenue, or the cost required to produce that revenue.

The most useful SaaS metrics combine leading indicators and lagging indicators. Activation, pipeline conversion, onboarding completion, and product engagement are leading indicators because they can signal future retention or revenue movement. MRR growth, churn, GRR, and NRR are lagging indicators because they report the outcome of customer behavior over time.

SaaS revenue metrics: MRR, ARR, growth rate, and expansion

Revenue growth metrics explain the size and direction of a recurring-revenue base. A consistent MRR bridge should separate new, expansion, contraction, and churned revenue so a headline growth number remains actionable.

Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)

What it measures: MRR is the normalized monthly revenue from active recurring subscriptions.

Formula: MRR = Sum of normalized monthly recurring subscription revenue

Why it matters: MRR provides a current view of recurring-revenue scale and is the foundation for most SaaS revenue reporting.

What can move it: New subscriptions, upgrades, downgrades, cancellations, plan changes, and reactivations can increase or decrease MRR.

Review alongside: MRR growth rate, new MRR, expansion MRR, contraction MRR, and churned MRR. Explore GrowthForge SaaS revenue and recurring-revenue insights for related reporting guidance.

Annual recurring revenue (ARR)

What it measures: ARR annualizes recurring subscription revenue. For businesses with monthly subscriptions, it is commonly calculated from current MRR.

Formula: ARR = MRR × 12

Why it matters: ARR is useful for annual planning and communicating recurring-revenue scale.

What can move it: The same events that change MRR will change ARR, including new business, expansion, contraction, and churn.

Review alongside: MRR, ARR growth, retention, and the monthly revenue bridge. Keep the definition of recurring revenue consistent when comparing periods.

MRR growth rate and new MRR

What it measures: MRR growth rate measures how quickly recurring revenue changes from one period to the next. New MRR isolates recurring revenue added from newly acquired customers.

Formula: MRR growth rate = (Ending MRR − Beginning MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: These SaaS revenue metrics show both top-line momentum and the contribution of new acquisition.

What can move it: Growth can rise through stronger acquisition, larger initial contracts, or expansion. It can fall because of weaker conversion, lower deal value, churn, or contraction.

Review alongside: Expansion MRR, churned MRR, CAC, trial-to-paid conversion, and NRR. MRR growth without the revenue bridge does not show whether the underlying model is becoming healthier.

Expansion MRR

What it measures: Expansion MRR is additional recurring revenue from existing customers through upgrades, more seats, higher usage, add-ons, or cross-sells.

Formula: Expansion MRR = MRR added from existing customers during the period

Why it matters: Expansion revenue is a key input to NRR and can show where customers are finding increasing value.

What can move it: Product adoption, account growth, packaging, pricing, customer success activity, and the availability of relevant upgrades can affect expansion.

Review alongside: Product engagement, GRR, NRR, ARPA, and customer segment performance. See GrowthForge customer retention and expansion strategy insights for related analysis.

SaaS retention metrics: customer churn, revenue churn, GRR, and NRR

Retention metrics distinguish customer losses from revenue losses and show whether an existing customer base is stable, shrinking, or expanding. Cohort views are especially useful because aggregate results can hide changing behavior among newer or higher-value customers.

Customer churn rate

What it measures: Customer churn measures the share of customers that cancel or fail to renew during a period.

Formula: Customer churn rate = Customers lost during period ÷ Customers at start of period × 100

Why it matters: Customer churn shows account loss and the pressure the business faces to replace customers before it can grow.

What can move it: Poor activation, weak product fit, pricing changes, service issues, changes in customer needs, or a different customer mix can affect churn.

Review alongside: Revenue churn, logo retention, cohort retention, activation, product engagement, and cancellation reasons. Use retention analytics and churn reduction resources to connect the metric to customer behavior.

Revenue churn rate

What it measures: Revenue churn measures recurring revenue lost from cancellations and, depending on the definition used, contractions during a period.

Formula: Revenue churn rate = Churned MRR during period ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: Revenue churn reveals the value of revenue lost, which customer-count churn can hide when higher-value accounts leave or downgrade.

What can move it: Losing larger accounts, downgrades, reduced usage, and changes in contract value can all alter revenue churn.

Review alongside: Customer churn, contraction MRR, GRR, NRR, and segment-level retention. Document whether contractions are included, then use the same definition across periods.

Gross revenue retention (GRR)

What it measures: GRR measures how much recurring revenue from the starting customer base remains after churn and contraction, before adding expansion.

Formula: GRR = (Beginning MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: GRR isolates baseline revenue retention because expansion revenue is excluded.

What can move it: Customer cancellations, downgraded plans, lower seat counts, and contracted usage can decrease GRR.

Review alongside: Revenue churn, NRR, logo retention, account cohorts, and product adoption. GRR is particularly useful when expansion could otherwise obscure underlying losses.

Net revenue retention (NRR)

What it measures: NRR measures how recurring revenue from the starting customer base changes after churn, contraction, and expansion.

Formula: NRR = (Beginning MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR + Expansion MRR) ÷ Beginning MRR × 100

Why it matters: NRR shows whether existing customers collectively generate more or less recurring revenue over time.

What can move it: NRR rises with expansion and falls with cancellations and contractions. Customer usage, account growth, pricing, packaging, and retention all influence it.
